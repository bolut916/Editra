How to make a print-ready PDF

Bleed, crop marks, resolution and CMYK: everything you need so your file isn’t rejected by the printer, on one page.

Step by step

  1. Add bleed (3 mm)Photos and colours that reach the edge must extend 3 mm beyond the trim line, so no white line is left after cutting.
  2. Stay in the safe areaText and logos must be at least 5 mm inside the trim line (10 mm on a magazine spine).
  3. Check the resolutionA photo must be at least 300 dpi at its printed size. Maths: pixels ÷ (mm ÷ 25.4). Example: 300 dpi across A4 width (210 mm) needs 2480 pixels.
  4. Convert to CMYKScreens are RGB, print is CMYK. Vivid greens and blues get slightly duller in print; be prepared.
  5. Crop marksThe PDF should have crop marks in the corners so the printer knows where to cut.
  6. In Vybble, in one place⋯ menu → Print Centre (Ctrl+Alt+X on a computer): turns on bleed and crop marks, flags low-resolution photos and downloads a CMYK PDF.

Tips

  • Use 100 % K black for black text; prefer rich black for large black areas.
  • Saddle-stitched jobs need a page count divisible by 4.
  • Make sure fonts are embedded in the PDF (Google Fonts can be embedded freely).
  • Ask your printer about their own requirements; some want 5 mm bleed.

Frequently asked questions

How much bleed should I add?

Most printers want 3 mm on each side. An A4 file with bleed is 216 × 303 mm.

What does 300 dpi mean?

300 pixels per printed inch (25.4 mm). Divide the photo’s pixels by the printed size in mm and multiply by 25.4.

What happens if I send an RGB file?

The printer converts it to CMYK and colours may look different from what you expected. Sending a CMYK PDF avoids surprises.

Are crop marks required?

Most printers ask for them. Vybble’s Print Centre adds them in one click.
